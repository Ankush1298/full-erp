## 2. Institutional data-control model (proposal)

Principles (to become the Institutional Data Protection Principles draft, `docs/legal-drafts/01`):

1. **Institution as controller, Anesa as processor** for institutional personal data, in most deployments (`security_artitecture.md` §compliance). Whether a given school is a Data Fiduciary and Anesa a Data Processor under the Indian Act is a question for counsel (§8), not an assumption.
2. **Purpose limitation by contract:** Anesa processes records only to provide the agreed service. No sale, no advertising, no profiling, no use to train models, no unrelated analytics, unless the institution gives a separate written authorization and the law allows it.
3. **No standing platform access:** platform-owner accounts hold no tenant data permission. Any access is by a time-boxed, justified, tenant-approved, audited grant (§4.4). Until that exists technically, the contract must say plainly that operators with infrastructure credentials _can_ technically reach data, and which procedures and records constrain them.
4. **Return and deletion on exit:** documented transition period, verified export, staged revocation, deletion/anonymization of eligible data, backup expiry window, completion certificate (§6).
5. **Separate "commitment" from "control":** every statement in a contract or policy is tagged _Contract_, _Process_ (human procedure with evidence) or _Technical_ (enforced by software and tested). Marketing may only repeat Technical claims that have a passing test cited in the verification column of §9.

## 4. Platform operator access assessment

Question: could Anesa's staff, an infrastructure administrator or a database superuser read, bypass or recover tenant data? **Short answer: yes, several ways, because the architecture protects tenants from each other and from application users, not from the party that operates the infrastructure.** The table states the technical boundary; none of these are blocked by software today.

| Capability                                                       | Possible?                                                                                                                       | Why                                                                                                                                                                             | What limits it today                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Read records across tenants through the application              | No for normal accounts                                                                                                          | RLS + authorization; no cross-tenant API; `tenant:create` etc. are CLI-only                                                                                                     | Authorization tests                                                                                            |
| Read records across tenants with the **platform CLI connection** | **Yes**                                                                                                                         | `cli.ts` uses `PLATFORM_DATABASE_URL`, `DATABASE_ADMIN_URL` or `MIGRATION_DATABASE_URL`: a privileged connection used to create tenants and seed roles; it is not tenant-scoped | Nothing but who holds the URL; CLI actions are not logged beyond tier-3 events for the new onboarding commands |

| Bypass RLS as the **table owner** (`app_migrator`)               | **Yes in practice**                                                                                                             | FORCE RLS applies to owners for ordinary queries, but an owner can `ALTER TABLE ... NO FORCE`, disable RLS or drop policies                                                     | `app_migrator` is "never used by the running application" (convention)                                         |

### 4.1 Honest summary for a principal

A school's data is protected from other schools and from other users of Anesa by software. It is **not** technically protected from a privileged Anesa operator or the hosting provider's administrators. Those risks are managed only by procedure, contract, and (not yet existing) evidence. Do not tell a school otherwise.

### 4.2 Safeguards to build or buy (recommendations, none approved)

1. **Separation of duties:** distinct credentials for migrator, operator, runtime, backup; no shared superuser; person-bound accounts; MFA on the cloud console; no standing production database access for developers.
2. **Privileged-access management:** time-boxed, ticketed, approved access to production data stores, session recording or statement logging shipped to storage the operator cannot edit.
3. **External audit evidence:** ship `audit_events` digests (hash chain or daily signed digest) to write-once storage; keep database/provider audit logs (e.g. pgaudit, cloud trail) outside the operator's control.
4. **Restricted database roles:** no `BYPASSRLS` except a single break-glass role whose use is alarmed; separate read-only support role bound to a tenant by `SECURITY DEFINER` functions; implement the designed `platform_admins` realm and tenant-approved support grants, then delete `support:grant`.
5. **Key custody:** a managed KMS with per-environment keys and access logs; consider per-tenant keys for documents (envelope encryption) so exit can include cryptographic erasure.
6. **Customer-controlled infrastructure** where a school or regulator requires technical exclusion of the operator (Option B, §7).
7. **Independent verification:** third-party penetration test and a design review of auth recovery, SSO and break-glass (already listed in `security_artitecture.md`), before real data.

## 8. Legal-readiness review: India DPDP (gap analysis, not a certification)

**Source reliability caveat.** The official PIB press release returned HTTP 403 and the notified Rules PDF could not be text-extracted in this environment, so the facts below come from secondary legal summaries returned by web search, not from the Gazette text. Dates and rule numbers must be confirmed against the official Gazette by counsel before use.

What the secondary sources report (to be verified):

- The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified on or about 13–14 November 2025 with **staggered commencement**: provisions on definitions, the Data Protection Board and government powers on notification; the consent-manager provisions (Rule 4) after **one year** (about 13–14 November 2026, i.e. days from this document); the substantive obligations (including consent notice, security safeguards, breach intimation, children's data, erasure, data principal rights, cross-border) after **eighteen months, about 13 May 2027**.
- In January 2026 MeitY reportedly considered shortening 18 months to 12; one source states no amending notification was found and mentions only a removal-of-difficulties order of 6 October 2026 affecting sections 9(1) and 10(2)(c)(ii). **Unverified.**
- Reported content (verify): reasonable security safeguards including encryption/logging and retention of logs for at least one year; breach intimation to the Board and affected individuals "without delay" with a detailed report within 72 hours; at least 48 hours' notice before erasure; a Fourth Schedule exemption for educational institutions that, on the narrower reading, covers tracking/behavioural monitoring for educational activities or safety of enrolled children; no targeted advertising to children; "child" means under 18.

### 9.1 Objective acceptance tests for real-data onboarding readiness

A school may be onboarded with real personal data only when **all** hold, with evidence filed:

1. Every P0 above is closed or has a written, owner-signed exception for the specific pilot.
2. Signed DPA and sub-processor disclosure matching the deployed configuration (script compares config to the list).
3. Production database roles: runtime `NOSUPERUSER NOBYPASSRLS`; no human holds standing superuser; privileged sessions logged to storage operators cannot edit (inspection + attempt drill).
4. Cross-tenant test suite passes against the production-like build, including `platform.*` tables (P1-1 may be accepted for pilot only with an explicit exception).
5. Restore drill from backup completed within the stated RTO; backups encrypted; key backup verified by a second person.
6. Export and deletion rehearsal for a synthetic tenant: counts match, checksum verified, no foreign-tenant rows, residual scan finds nothing, completion certificate generated.
7. Incident tabletop done; breach clock owners named.
8. MFA mandatory for all administrators (already enforced) and first-administrator onboarding by the operator runbook (doc 45 §8) with local-only token handling.
9. Counsel's written opinion on children's data and exemptions; notice/consent text approved.
10. No policy or marketing statement exceeds the Technical column of §1.
